§5.1

Management Commitment

Top management must provide evidence of commitment to developing, implementing, and continually improving the QMS. Commitment is demonstrated through actions — not declarations.

  • Communicate the importance of meeting customer and regulatory requirements
  • Establish a Quality Policy appropriate to the organisation's purpose
  • Ensure measurable quality objectives are established at all relevant levels
  • Conduct Management Reviews at planned intervals
  • Ensure the availability of resources necessary for the QMS
§5.2

Customer Focus

Top management must ensure that customer requirements and applicable regulatory requirements are determined and consistently met. This is not delegated — it requires active management monitoring.

  • Identify and document customer requirements, including post-delivery requirements
  • Ensure applicable regulatory requirements for each market are identified and met
  • Monitor performance against customer satisfaction data, complaint trends, and quality objectives
  • Take action when customer requirements or quality objectives are not being met
§5.3

Quality Policy

The Quality Policy must be appropriate to the organisation's purpose, include a commitment to meeting requirements and continual improvement, and provide a framework for quality objectives. It must be communicated, understood, and reviewed at each Management Review.

It must reference ALL markets in which the organisation places devices on the market.

  • Reviewed and approved by top management at each Management Review
  • Communicated and understood at all levels of the organisation
  • Aligned with regulatory requirements for every jurisdiction in which devices are registered
  • Updated whenever the organisation enters a new market or a regulatory change affects scope
§5.4.1

Quality Objectives

Measurable quality objectives must be established at relevant functions and levels. They must be consistent with the quality policy. When an objective is not met, management must formally document a response — recording the failure alone is not sufficient.

  • Objectives must be measurable — expressed as a specific, quantifiable target (e.g. percentage, rate, or threshold)
  • Established at relevant functions and levels within the organisation — not only at the corporate level
  • Reviewed at each Management Review with documented performance data
  • Where an objective has not been met, top management must formally document a response — including an analysis of the gap and a committed action or resource decision
§5.5

Responsibility, Authority & Communication

Responsibilities and authorities must be defined, documented, and communicated throughout the organisation. When roles change or are eliminated, responsibilities must be formally transferred — not informally assumed.

  • All QMS roles must have documented responsibilities and authorities
  • The Management Representative must be appointed by top management and must report QMS performance at each Management Review
  • Communication processes for QMS effectiveness must be established throughout the organisation (§5.5.3)
  • When a role that holds QMS responsibilities is changed, eliminated, or handed over, the transfer of those responsibilities must be documented and verified
  • No QMS responsibility should remain unassigned at any time
§5.6

Management Review — §5.6.1 / §5.6.2 / §5.6.3

Top management must review the QMS at planned intervals to ensure its continuing suitability, adequacy, and effectiveness. Records must be maintained.

§5.6.2 — Required inputs include:

  • Customer feedback and complaint trends
  • Reporting to regulatory authorities
  • Audit findings (internal and external — including BSI)
  • Monitoring of processes and product conformity
  • Status of corrective and preventive actions (CAPAs)
  • Follow-up actions from previous Management Reviews
  • Planned changes affecting the QMS
  • New or revised regulatory requirements
  • Recommendations for improvement

§5.6.3 — Required outputs: Decisions on QMS improvements, product improvements, and resource needs. Resource decisions made during or after Management Reviews must be documented.

Records of Management Reviews must be maintained as QMS records. Minutes must accurately reflect the inputs discussed and the outputs — including any decisions not to act, and the rationale.

§6.1

Provision of Resources

"The organization shall determine and provide the resources needed to: (a) implement the quality management system and to maintain its effectiveness; (b) meet applicable regulatory and customer requirements."

This clause places a direct obligation on top management. Resource decisions cannot be made independently of QMS obligations. The standard does not permit an organisation to operate an inadequately resourced QMS and remain compliant.

  • Resources include personnel, infrastructure, work environment, and information systems
  • Top management must determine what resources are needed — this requires an assessment, not an assumption
  • Before reducing QA/RA resources, management must assess the impact on QMS processes and responsibilities
  • Where resource constraints are identified, a documented escalation pathway must exist so that concerns are formally recorded and acted upon
  • Evidence of resource provision decisions must be retained as QMS records
§6.2

Human Resources & Competency

Personnel performing QMS work must be competent on the basis of appropriate education, training, skills, and experience. The organisation must:

  • Determine the competency requirements for each QMS role
  • Provide training or other actions to achieve the required competence
  • Evaluate the effectiveness of training — a training attendance record is not sufficient
  • Document education, training, skills, and experience for all relevant personnel
§6.3

Infrastructure

The organisation must determine, provide, and maintain the infrastructure needed to achieve product conformity. This includes buildings and workspace, process equipment, and supporting services including information systems (ERP, eQMS).

  • The eQMS must be maintained and all controlled documents managed within it — uncontrolled document storage is not acceptable for QMS-critical records
  • ERP and other systems must have appropriate access controls — only authorised personnel should be able to assign or change product release status
  • Infrastructure must be regularly reviewed to ensure it continues to support QMS requirements
  • Maintenance plans must be documented and implemented to prevent infrastructure failure affecting product quality or QMS processes
